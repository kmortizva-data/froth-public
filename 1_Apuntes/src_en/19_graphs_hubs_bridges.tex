\section{What a graph is}

\begin{concepto}{graph $=$ things $+$ relations}
Think of your social network: people $=$ \textbf{nodes}, friendships $=$ \textbf{edges} (the
lines). That structure, things connected by relations, is a \textbf{graph}, and it sits behind
Google Maps (junctions $+$ streets), LinkedIn (people $+$ contacts) and our mind map (papers
$+$ ``are related to''). The standard library in Python: \codigo{networkx}.
\end{concepto}

Four ideas we will use:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Weight}: not all relations are equally strong. ``They match at 0.94'' weighs
        more than ``they match at 0.60'', and the line is drawn thicker.
  \item \textbf{Direction}: similarity is mutual (an edge with no arrow); citations are not,
        A cites B, not the other way round (an edge \emph{with} an arrow).
  \item \textbf{Hub}: a node with a great many connections.
  \item \textbf{Bridge}: a node or edge connecting two communities that barely touch.
\end{enumerate}

\section{What each one is for in YOUR literature review}

\begin{concepto}{hubs $=$ foundation; bridges $=$ originality}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{The hub} is the compulsory reading: the classic everyone cites. It goes in the
        introduction of your review, it shows you know the ground. But \emph{everybody knows
        the hubs}: there is no novelty possible there.
  \item \textbf{The bridge} connects two topics that barely speak to each other. If only 2
        papers join ``flotation'' with ``deposit geology'', that frontier is under-explored
        $\rightarrow$ a new contribution fits there. The \emph{gaps} of Phase 5 are, literally,
        missing bridges.
\end{itemize}
Short answer: hubs to ground yourself, \textbf{bridges to contribute}.
\end{concepto}

\begin{ojo}
\textbf{Your thesis IS a bridge.} Look at the title: \emph{``Flotation of ... Li bearing mica
minerals \textbf{and its impact on} the by-products (Ta, Nb, Be,...) recovery...''}. It
connects exactly the green cluster (flotation) with the blue one (geology / granite
by-products). It is not a topic \emph{inside} a community: it is the \emph{frontier} between
two. That is why it is a thesis and not an assignment. When the network is built, look for
yourself in it.
\end{ojo}

\section{Which graph we will build (what comes next)}

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Similarity network} (4.2): each paper connected to its k most similar
        neighbours by cosine \textbf{in 768D} (the real space, not the 2D map, see Note 12).
        With a threshold, so it does not come out as a ``hairball''.
  \item \textbf{Citation network} (4.3): arrows for who cites whom. It requires re-downloading
        the papers asking for the \codigo{referenced\_works} field that we did not request in
        Phase 1.
  \item \textbf{Topic mind map} (4.4): one node per subtopic, edges $=$ how related they are.
        The presentable view.
\end{enumerate}

\begin{resumen}
A graph is nodes $+$ edges (with weight and sometimes direction). Hubs are the classics that
ground you; bridges are the frontiers where originality lives, and your own thesis is a bridge
between flotation and geology. We will build similarity, citation and topic networks.
\end{resumen}
