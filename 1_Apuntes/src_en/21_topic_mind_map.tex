\section{Going up a level: from papers to topics}

The network of Note 20 has 120 nodes: good for exploring, bad for \emph{presenting}. The
\textbf{topic mind map} aggregates the information one level up: each \textbf{whole subtopic
becomes ONE node} (size $=$ number of papers) and the edges summarise the relation between
subtopics. It is the executive view: 3 or 4 bubbles that tell the whole field.

\begin{concepto}{aggregation: the same network, less resolution}
This trick, grouping nodes and summarising their relations, is called \emph{aggregation} and
it is universal: from people $\rightarrow$ departments, from streets $\rightarrow$ cities, from
papers $\rightarrow$ subtopics. You lose detail, you gain legibility. That is why we keep
\emph{both} networks: the paper one (to explore) and the topic one (to understand and
present).
\end{concepto}

\section{The edges carry TWO numbers (and that is the point)}

Between each pair of subtopics we measure two different things:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Similarity} $=$ the average cosine between the papers of one and the other (in
        768D). ``How related is what they \emph{study}?''
  \item \textbf{Cross-citations} $=$ how many times a paper in one subtopic \emph{cites} one in
        the other. ``How much do they \emph{read} each other?''
\end{enumerate}

\section{The finding: silos (your gap, quantified)}

With your data this came out:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lcc}
\textbf{Relation} & \textbf{Similarity} & \textbf{Cross-citations} \\
\hline
Geology $\leftrightarrow$ Flotation & 0.688 & \textbf{1} \\
Geology $\leftrightarrow$ Economy/recycling & 0.662 & 3 \\
Flotation $\leftrightarrow$ Economy/recycling & 0.683 & 1 \\
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\begin{ojo}
Read the first row with a thesis writer's eyes: geology and flotation \textbf{study very
related things} (0.69 is high) but \textbf{barely cite each other}, 1 citation among 90
papers! Of the corpus's 116 internal citations, only 5 cross subtopic borders: the communities
are \textbf{silos}, connected by meaning, separated by habit.

Your thesis (``flotation of Li micas \emph{and its impact on} the recovery of granite
by-products'') lives exactly in that silence between silos. This is a \emph{gap} that is
quantified and citable in your introduction: Phase 5 will formalise this detection.
\end{ojo}

\section{Where it lives in the code}

prints when you run \codigo{python -m froth.graph}; the preview is in

\begin{resumen}
Aggregating the paper network up to topic level gives the executive view (3 or 4 bubbles).
Each edge carries similarity (what they study) and citations (what they read), and in your
corpus they diverge: closely related subtopics that do not cite each other $=$ silos $=$ your
gap, already with numbers.
\end{resumen}
