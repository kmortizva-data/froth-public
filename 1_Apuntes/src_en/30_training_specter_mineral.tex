\cabecera{30}{The birth of specter-mineral: your first real training run}

\section{What happened (in 83 seconds)}

Your RTX 5060 took generic SPECTER and \textbf{fine-tuned} it with the $\sim$1,250
title$\leftrightarrow$abstract pairs of the master corpus: 2 epochs, 160 steps, batches of 16.
The result lives in \codigo{models/specter-mineral}, an embedding model that speaks the dialect
of mineral processing. On CPU it would have been hours; on your GPU, 83 seconds.

\section{The concepts you have just used}

\begin{concepto}{fine-tuning $\neq$ training from scratch}
We did not build a new model: we took SPECTER's weights (which already know how to ``read
papers'') and \textbf{nudged them gently} towards your domain. Like a generalist geologist
taking a specialisation, they do not go back to primary school.
\end{concepto}

\begin{concepto}{the elegant move: self-supervision with MNRL}
Training usually demands human labels (``these two texts are alike''). We labelled NOTHING. The
trick: \textbf{MultipleNegativesRankingLoss}. In each batch of 16 papers, a paper's title must
end up closer to \emph{its own} abstract than to the other 15 in the batch. Free positives (a
paper's title and abstract talk about the same thing) and free negatives (the rest of the
batch). A flat corpus becomes a training dataset without lifting a finger.
\end{concepto}

\begin{concepto}{the vocabulary of training}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{loss}: the number measuring ``how wrong the model is''; training $=$ pushing the
        weights to bring it down.
  \item \textbf{step}: processing one batch and adjusting the weights once. You saw ``160 steps''.
  \item \textbf{epoch}: one complete pass over the whole dataset. We did 2.
  \item \textbf{warmup}: the first steps with very gentle adjustments, so as not to wreck what
        the model already knew.
  \item \textbf{mixed precision (amp)}: arithmetic in lower-precision numbers where it does not
        matter, part of the reason for the speed on GPU.
\end{itemize}
\end{concepto}

\begin{ojo}
\textbf{The stumbles teach too}: the training failed TWICE before it started. First
\codigo{datasets} was missing, then \codigo{accelerate} (the Hugging Face Trainer's kit).
Ecosystem lesson: in modern ML, ``installing the library'' is rarely enough; the training stack
is its own beast. Both went into requirements.txt for good. And before that: pip refused to
swap torch CPU$\rightarrow$CUDA because ``the version was already satisfied'' (same number,
different variant), solved with \codigo{--force-reinstall --no-deps}.
\end{ojo}

\section{How we judge whether it was worth it (6.6)}

A new model is not better just for existing. \codigo{froth/compare.py} puts BOTH models
through the same exam over the 22 topics: same corpora, same UMAP (fixed seed), granularity
chosen by DBCV. Who produces more valid clusters? It also exports a \textbf{blind test}: two
A/B cluster listings with the key hidden, so the expert (you) can judge without knowing which
is which. If specter-mineral does not win, that is ALSO an honest and publishable result.

\begin{resumen}
Fine-tuning $=$ pushing existing weights towards your domain; MNRL turns the corpus into a
dataset with no labelling (your title must love your abstract more than other people's); 83s on
your GPU after two dependency stumbles. The verdict comes from DBCV over 22 topics and your
blind test, not from enthusiasm.
\end{resumen}
