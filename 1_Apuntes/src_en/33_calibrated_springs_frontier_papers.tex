\cabecera{33}{Calibrated springs and frontier papers: what position means in the network}

\section{Your question: ``do the springs mean anything?''}

In the Network view the nodes arrange themselves with a \textbf{force layout} (forceAtlas2):
each edge is a spring pulling things together, and all the nodes repel each other like charges.
The equilibrium of that tug of war decides the position. By default vis.js uses the SAME spring
length for every edge, so visual distance was pure aesthetics.

\section{The calibration: length $=$ dissimilarity}

Our edges already carry a weight $w$ $=$ cosine similarity (with a 0.75 threshold). Now each
spring is made to measure:
\[
\mathrm{length} = 60 + 420\,(1 - w)
\]
Nearly identical neighbours ($w \to 1$) end up $\sim$60 px apart; the ones that barely passed
the threshold, $\sim$165 px. Since that change, \textbf{close on screen $\approx$ close in
meaning}. The network answers the same question as the UMAP map, but with the connections made
explicit.

\section{The mystery of the ``yellow node stuck to the blue hub''}

You noticed nodes of one colour sitting inside another's territory. It was not a bug: they were
\textbf{frontier papers}. The chain of events:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item HDBSCAN cuts the clusters on the \textbf{2D} UMAP map.
  \item The network connects neighbours in the original \textbf{768D}.
  \item A paper can fall on the yellow side of the 2D cut while having almost all its semantic
        neighbours in the blue cluster $\rightarrow$ the springs drag it towards the blue.
\end{enumerate}

\begin{concepto}{frontier paper detection}
For each node we count its neighbours' clusters. If $\geq 60\%$ belong to ANOTHER cluster, it
is a frontier paper: it is drawn with a \textbf{dashed ring in the neighbouring cluster's
colour} and the tooltip declares it (``bridges into the neighboring subtopic''). In Beauvoir:
3 out of 237 papers.
\end{concepto}

\begin{ojo}
Frontier papers are not clustering errors: they tend to be the interdisciplinary ones, exactly
those connecting subtopics. For a reader hunting research gaps, they are among the most
interesting on the map.
\end{ojo}

\begin{resumen}
Springs with length $= 60 + 420(1-w)$: visual proximity now IS semantic distance. The
``misplaced'' nodes are frontier papers (most of their neighbours in another cluster), now
marked with a dashed ring in the neighbour's colour.
\end{resumen}
