\cabecera{27}{Recommended granularity: let the data choose (DBCV)}

\section{The question that started this}

``I want a \emph{set to recommended} button with a REAL statistical reason: not a standard
value, but one computed every time.'' Exactly the right attitude: fixed defaults are opinions;
the data changes with every corpus.

\section{The right metric: DBCV}

\begin{concepto}{DBCV (Density-Based Cluster Validity)}
To evaluate a \emph{density-based} clustering (HDBSCAN) not just any metric will do: the famous
\emph{silhouette}, for instance, assumes round, compact clusters. \textbf{DBCV} is the family's
native metric: it rewards clusterings whose groups are \textbf{dense inside} and
\textbf{well separated outside}, respecting irregular shapes.
In code: \codigo{hdbscan.HDBSCAN(gen\_min\_span\_tree=True).relative\_validity\_}.
\end{concepto}

\begin{concepto}{the sweep}
\codigo{cluster.recommend\_min\_cluster\_size()} tries EVERY granularity from 3 to 20, computes
the DBCV validity of each, discards the degenerate ones (a single cluster is not a clustering)
and returns the winner plus a diagnostic table. With today's enlarged corpus: recommended
$= 7$ (validity 0.51). If the corpus changes tomorrow, it recomputes by itself (cached by data
version).
\end{concepto}

\begin{ojo}
The recommendation is a \emph{statistical optimum}, not a final truth: it optimises density and
separation, not ``which story serves the expert''. That is why the slider is still there,
wandering is allowed and the button always brings you back. Machine proposes; human disposes
(the pattern of all of Froth).
\end{ojo}

\begin{resumen}
The ``Use recommended'' button does not store a magic number: it sweeps granularities and picks
the one maximising DBCV, the native quality metric of density-based clustering, recomputed for
each corpus. Real statistics, with the slider free for exploring.
\end{resumen}
