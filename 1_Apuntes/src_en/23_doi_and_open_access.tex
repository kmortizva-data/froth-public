\section{The new feature: from ranking to reading}

The search engine already ranked papers; now it also takes you to \textbf{read} them: each
result carries two links, \textbf{DOI} (the paper's official page) and \textbf{open PDF} (the
free PDF, when it exists). In your corpus: \textbf{85 of 126 papers (67\%) have an open access
PDF} and 116 of 126 have a DOI.

\begin{concepto}{DOI (Digital Object Identifier)}
The \textbf{DOI} is a paper's permanent ``ID number'': \codigo{10.1016/j...}. The URL
\codigo{https://doi.org/[DOI]} always leads to the publisher's official page, even if the
journal changes website. It is the correct way to cite and to reach the text; if the paper is
paywalled, that is where you use your university access.
\end{concepto}

\begin{concepto}{open access (OA)}
A paper is \textbf{open access} when a legally free copy exists: in the journal itself, in
repositories (arXiv, HAL...) or on the author's university page. OpenAlex tracks where that
copy is and gives us the URL (\codigo{oa\_url}). We only asked for one more field in the
pull's \codigo{select} (\codigo{open\_access}), one line of code, and the pipeline regenerated
everything else by itself.
\end{concepto}

\begin{ojo}
\textbf{Linking yes, mass downloading no.} Froth gives you the LINK and you click it: the
reading happens on your side, with your permissions. Downloading PDFs en masse from publishers
violates their terms of use (a risk foreseen in the plan from day 1). The serious tools
(Connected Papers and the like) do exactly this same thing: rank $+$ link.
\end{ojo}

\section{Bonus of the day: Streamlit's slow watchman}

The app took a long time to start and the log filled up with \codigo{torchvision} warnings.
Diagnosis: Streamlit's \emph{file watcher} (auto-reload when you edit code) tries to inspect
the hundreds of \codigo{transformers} modules, one by one. Fix: turn it off in
\codigo{.streamlit/config.toml} (\codigo{fileWatcherType = "none"}). Double lesson: (1) a noisy
log is not always a fatal error, you have to read WHAT it says; (2) comfortable development
tools sometimes cost performance.

\begin{resumen}
Every search result now links to the DOI (official page) and to the open access PDF when one
exists (67\% of your corpus). We link, we do not mass download. And the app starts faster after
turning off Streamlit's file watcher.
\end{resumen}
