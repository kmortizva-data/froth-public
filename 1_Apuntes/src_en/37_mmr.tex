\cabecera{37}{MMR: relevant but different (and an objective that came out degenerate)}

\section{The problem that remained}

The centroid (note 35) has a structural flaw: sentences resembling the centre resemble EACH
OTHER. In your geology cluster, the 3 best sentences had a mutual similarity of \textbf{0.945},
a summary saying the same thing three times.

\begin{concepto}{Maximal Marginal Relevance (Carbonell \& Goldstein, 1998)}
Greedy selection, one sentence at a time. Each candidate is scored as
\[
\lambda \cdot \mathrm{relevance} \;-\; (1-\lambda) \cdot \mathrm{redundancy}
\]
where redundancy $=$ its maximum similarity to what is ALREADY chosen. With $\lambda = 1$ you
are back to the pure centroid; lowering $\lambda$ buys diversity by paying in relevance. The
first one chosen is always the most relevant (nothing has been chosen yet to be redundant
with).
\end{concepto}

\section{The stumble (which teaches more than the success)}

First attempt at choosing $\lambda$ from the data: ``maximise relevance $-$ redundancy''.
Result: it recommended $\lambda = 0.5$... the EDGE of the grid. That objective is
\textbf{degenerate}: lowering $\lambda$ ALWAYS reduces redundancy faster than it loses
relevance, so the ``optimum'' runs off downwards indefinitely. A recommender that always picks
the extreme is measuring nothing.

\section{The rule that does work}

Looking at your real sweep (Beauvoir corpus, 12 clusters):

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{cccc}
$\lambda$ & relevance & redundancy \\
\hline
0.5 & 0.868 & 0.758 \\
0.6 & 0.906 & 0.829 \\
\textbf{0.7} & \textbf{0.918} & \textbf{0.860} \\
0.8 & 0.921 & 0.873 \\
1.0 & 0.923 & 0.884 \\
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

From 1.0 down to 0.7 relevance barely falls ($-0.5\%$) and the diversity comes for free; from
0.7 downwards you start really paying. Rule adopted (interpretable): \textbf{the most diverse
$\lambda$ that keeps $\geq 99\%$ of the achievable relevance}. Your corpus voted $\lambda = 0.7$
on its own, and it coincides with the literature's default, but now we KNOW why it holds for
this data (project rule 9).

\begin{practica}
\texttt{.venv\textbackslash Scripts\textbackslash python.exe -m froth.review}: at the end it
prints the sweep and the before/after of the most redundant cluster. The centroid gives 3
cloned pegmatite sentences; MMR keeps the definition and adds Nb-Ta granites and the
Erzgebirge. Three angles, not an echo.
\end{practica}

\begin{ojo}
MMR is greedy: it picks the best NOW without looking ahead, because the exact optimum is
combinatorial (trying every combination of sentences). For 3 to 5 sentence summaries, the
greedy approximation is the standard and it is more than enough.
\end{ojo}

\begin{resumen}
MMR $=$ $\lambda \cdot$relevance $-$ $(1-\lambda) \cdot$redundancy, greedy selection. The first
objective for choosing $\lambda$ came out degenerate (it stuck to the edge); the final rule,
maximum diversity keeping 99\% of the relevance, chose 0.7 with your data. That completes the 3
ranking pieces: typical (centroid), endorsed (TextRank) and diverse (MMR). Next comes
assembling draft v2 (7.4).
\end{resumen}
